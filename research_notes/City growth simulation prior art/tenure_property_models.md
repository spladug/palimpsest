# Land tenure and property-rights systems in spatial simulations of settlement growth

Scope: annotated bibliography for a browser-based, vector-geometry town-growth simulation with pluggable tenure regimes (burgage, open field/commons/enclosure, US PLSS/railroad plats/speculation, Mexican ejido incl. 1992 reform). Parcels carry a "bundle of rights" held by entities, with regime-change events.

Verification note: items marked [verified] had their bibliographic details confirmed via search results in this session. Items marked [details from search snippet only] are confirmed to exist but the content summary relies on catalogue/abstract snippets, not full-text reading. Several full-text fetches failed (MDPI, IJURR, HUD PDF, TUM repository), so no page-level quotes were taken from those.

## Q1. Spatial / agent-based models that explicitly represent tenure, property rights, commons or land markets

### Takeaway
Land-market ABMs (Parker & Filatova; ALMA; CHALMS) give well-documented mechanisms for bid/ask price formation, developer/farmer/household agents and speculative price expectations, but treat property as simple freehold transferable plots; informal-settlement ABMs (Augustijn-Beckers et al.; Patel et al.) give micro-scale, vector-based house/plot growth rules and political actors, but tenure is mostly implicit. No ABM found that represents a decomposable "bundle of rights" across holder types with regime changes, and no ejido-specific ABM/CA was found — this is the main gap the project would fill.

### Cited Findings
- **Parker, D.C. & Filatova, T. (2008). "A conceptual design for a bilateral agent-based land market with heterogeneous economic agents." *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* 32(6): 454–463.** [verified] Design includes multiple buyers and sellers (households, developers, rural landowners), price expectations formed from local neighbourhoods, spatial externalities, and alternative models of bid/ask formation and division of gains from trade. Relevance: borrow the bilateral bid/ask transaction protocol for parcels whose "sell" right is held; gap — rights are undivided freehold. — [MSU summary](https://www.canr.msu.edu/resources/conceptual-design-bilateral-agent-based-land-market-heterogeneous-economic-agents)
- **Filatova, T., Parker, D. & van der Veen, A. (2009). "Agent-Based Urban Land Markets: Agent's Pricing Behavior, Land Prices and Urban Land Use Change." *JASSS* 12(1): 3.** [verified] The ALMA (Agent-based Land MArket) model explicitly models behavioural drivers of transactions on both buyer and seller sides, formation of bid and ask prices, and division of gains from trade; prices and land-use patterns emerge bottom-up. Relevance: open-access, implementable reference for a market module plugged in only when the active tenure regime grants alienability (e.g. post-1992 ejido dominio pleno, US fee-simple lots). — [JASSS](https://www.jasss.org/12/1/3.html)
- **Magliocca, N.R., Safirova, E., McConnell, V. & Walls, M. (2011). "An economic agent-based model of coupled housing and land markets (CHALMS)." *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* 35(3): 183–191.** [verified citation via CoMSES] Spatially explicit model of an ex-urban area with consumer, farmer and developer agents; farmland is converted to housing through linked land and housing markets, with developers and farmers using adaptive price expectations. Relevance: the farmer-as-holdout and developer price-expectation logic is the closest available model of speculative lot holding (useful for railroad-town/town-company speculation); code is on CoMSES. Gap: no tenure heterogeneity. — [CoMSES publication](https://catalog.comses.net/publications/2419); [model code](https://www.comses.net/codebases/3368)
- **Augustijn-Beckers, E.-W., Flacke, J. & Retsios, B. (2011). "Simulating informal settlement growth in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania: An agent-based housing model." *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* 35(2): 93–103. DOI 10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2011.01.001.** [verified] A vector-based, micro-scale housing ABM built on three spatial-change rules — infilling, extension and enlargement of existing houses — that reproduces informal-settlement housing patterns. Relevance: directly analogous to the project's vector-geometry approach; the infill/extension/enlargement rules map well onto encroachment on commons/waste and onto ejido "irregular" subdivision. Gap: tenure (customary/informal claims) not modelled as rights. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2011.01.001); [CoMSES](https://catalog.comses.net/publications/247)
- **Patel, A., Crooks, A. & Koizumi, N. (2012). "Slumulation: An Agent-Based Modeling Approach to Slum Formations." *JASSS* 15(4): 2.** [verified] NetLogo ABM with household, developer and politician agents; multi-scale environment of housing units within electoral wards; slums emerge from human–environment interaction and inter-scale feedbacks. Relevance: borrow the "politician/state" agent as a holder of tolerance/regularisation powers (cf. Mexican PRI-era clientelist regularisation of ejido settlements). — [JASSS](https://www.jasss.org/15/4/2.html); [PDF](https://css1.gmu.edu/Slums/PatelCrooksKoizumi_Slumulation.pdf)
- **Janssen, M.A. & Ostrom, E. (2006). "Empirically Based, Agent-based models." *Ecology and Society* 11(2).** [title/venue verified from search snippet] Methodological paper on grounding commons ABMs in field/experimental data; Janssen and Ostrom also co-wrote "Governing Social-Ecological Systems" in the *Handbook of Computational Economics* vol. 2 (2006), and the book *Working Together* (Princeton, 2010). Relevance: framework for calibrating commons-use rules (stinting, encroachment sanctions) on open fields/waste. Gap: these are resource-harvest models, not spatial plot-tenure models. — [Janssen ABM page](https://marcojanssen.info/agent-based-modeling); [bibliographic record of 2006 chapter](https://www-iuem.univ-brest.fr/epure/bibliographie-generale/janssen_chapter_2006)
- Customary/African tenure ABMs found are agricultural, not settlement-form models: e.g. an ABM of South African land redistribution built from surveys of 658 commercial farmers and 833 smallholders, simulating willing-seller/willing-buyer, subdivision and expropriation scenarios. Relevance: "subdivide" and "expropriate" as regime-change events. — [Orgprints PDF](https://orgprints.org/id/eprint/56119/1/South%20Africa%20s%20land%20redistribution%20an%20agent-based%20model%20for%20assessing%20structural%20and%20economic%20impacts.pdf); also an Ethiopian large-scale-land-acquisition smallholder ABM — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/let-the-farmer-decide-examining-smallholder-autonomy-in-large-sca/)

### Inferences
- A practical architecture: tenure regime decides which rights exist and who holds them; a market module (ALMA-style bid/ask) only runs over parcels whose "sell" right is held by an individual; an informal-growth module (Augustijn-Beckers rules) runs where use/build rights are de facto claimed without formal title.
- Speculative lot holding can be modelled with CHALMS-style adaptive price expectations: owners withhold land when expected future price exceeds current bid.

### Gaps
- No ABM/CA found that models Mexican ejido urbanisation specifically (a search on CA/ABM + ejido returned only generic Mexican CA models, e.g. Mexico City vulnerable-area CA and an Ensenada urban growth simulator). — [search result example](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1380772/)
- No ABM found representing medieval burgage, open-field strips or enclosure; no model found with a decomposable bundle of rights across multiple holders. Searches were limited (~18 tool calls) so absence is suggestive, not conclusive.
- Exact DOIs for Patel et al. (JASSS) and Magliocca et al. (CEUS) not confirmed; use the URLs above.

## Q2. Ejido urbanisation empirical literature and Spanish colonial planning

### Takeaway
Post-1992 ejido urbanisation is better described as deregulation and partial, often informal, incorporation than wholesale privatisation; for a model this means regime change should alter who may sell/subdivide but conversion should remain slow, patchy and frequently informal. The Laws of the Indies supply a canonical initial geometry (plaza-centred grid with surrounding commons/ejido and fields).

### Cited Findings
- **Jones, G.A. & Ward, P.M. (1998). "Privatizing the commons: reforming the ejido and urban development in Mexico." *International Journal of Urban and Regional Research* 22(1): 76–93. DOI 10.1111/1468-2427.00124.** [verified] Examines the 1991–92 Article 27 reforms; argues they amount to deregulation of former tenure relations rather than outright privatisation, with administrative power over ejido land shifting from the Agrarian Reform Ministry toward the Social Development Ministry and local government. Relevance: regime-change event should transfer "regulate/approve conversion" rights from federal agrarian to municipal entities, not simply flip parcels to freehold. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-2427.00124); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/ijurrs/v22y1998i1p76-93.html)
- **Jones, G.A. & Ward, P.M. (2000?). "Public–private partnerships for urban land development in Mexico: a victory for hope versus expectation?" *Habitat International*.** [existence verified; year from snippet stated as 2000] Assesses partnerships on ejido land by whether they increased land supply, improved low-income access and market efficiency. — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/44775)
- **"The Impact of Mexico's Land Reform on Periurban Housing Production: Neoliberal or Neocorporatist?" (IJURR).** [existence verified; authors/year not confirmed in this session] — [IJURR page](https://www.ijurr.org/article/the-impact-of-mexicos-land-reform-on-periurban-housing-production-neoliberal-or-neocorporatist/)
- **Schumacher, M., Durán-Díaz, P., Kurjenoja, A.K., Gutiérrez-Juárez, E. & González-Rivas, D.A. (2019). "Evolution and Collapse of Ejidos in Mexico—To What Extent Is Communal Land Used for Urban Development?" *Land* 8(10): 146. DOI 10.3390/land8100146.** [verified citation; open access] Snippet-level finding: despite legal changes, the reforms did not translate into massive privatisation of ejido land, and incorporation of social land into urban development is far below expectations. Relevance: calibrate low conversion rates and persistence of communal parcels inside a growing urban fringe. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/land8100146); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jlands/v8y2019i10p146-d274048.html)
- PROCEDE (Programa de Certificación de Derechos Ejidales y Titulación de Solares Urbanos), grounded in the Article 27 reform published 6 January 1992, certified individual rights to farm plots, common land and urban house-lots (solares) and was administered by the Procuraduría Agraria. Relevance: the three categories (parcela, uso común, solar urbano) map directly onto three parcel types in the ejido regime. — [ERA anthropology resource](https://era.anthropology.ac.uk/Era_Resources/Era/Peasants/mexcase16a.html); Spanish-language study on PROCEDE/PISO and change of use in the Mexico City metropolitan zone — [Estudios Demográficos y Urbanos](https://estudiosdemograficosyurbanos.colmex.mx/index.php/edu/en/article/view/1189)
- **Laws of the Indies (Ordinances of 1573).** 148 ordinances on site selection, street and square layout, placement of civic/religious buildings, open space, cultivation and pasture lands; plan centred on a Plaza Mayor of prescribed size with streets in a rectilinear grid. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_the_Indies); [HUD translation PDF](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Laws-of-the-Indies.pdf) (fetch failed; ordinance numbers for ejido/solar clauses not verified)
- **Crouch, D.P., Garr, D.J. & Mundigo, A.I. (1982). *Spanish City Planning in North America*. MIT Press.** [existence verified; year/publisher from general knowledge] Covers ~350 new cities built under the Laws of the Indies, with translation of the ordinances. — [CCA catalogue](https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/search/details/library/publication/07945102)

### Inferences
- Model ejido as three zones: zona urbana (solares, heritable, quasi-private), parcelas (individual usufruct, inheritable within ejido, not alienable pre-1992), tierras de uso común (assembly-controlled). 1992 event adds conditional "sell to outsider" via dominio pleno after assembly vote, and grows informal sales (Augustijn-Beckers-style infill) at the urban edge.

### Gaps
- Ward's *Mexico City* (book) and Olivera's work on ejido urbanisation not verified in this session.
- No quantitative conversion rates were extracted (full text of Schumacher et al. not fetched).
- Distinction between colonial "ejido" (town commons in the Laws of the Indies) and the 20th-century agrarian ejido should be noted; not sourced here beyond the general summaries.

## Q3. Conceptual frameworks: bundle of rights and property rights vs urban form

### Takeaway
Schlager & Ostrom's nested rights classes give a ready data model for the parcel "bundle"; Webster & Lai give the theory linking rights fragmentation and transaction costs to emergent spatial order.

### Cited Findings
- **Schlager, E. & Ostrom, E. (1992). "Property-Rights Regimes and Natural Resources: A Conceptual Analysis." *Land Economics* 68(3): 249–262. DOI 10.2307/3146375.** [citation verified; DOI from general knowledge] Distinguishes bundles of rights — access, withdrawal, management, exclusion, alienation — held cumulatively by authorized users, claimants, proprietors and owners. Relevance: adopt the five rights plus the project's build/subdivide/inherit as fields on each parcel, with holder types mapping to positions. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/uwp/landec/v68y1992i3p249-262.html); [open PDF (Indiana DLC)](https://dlc.dlib.indiana.edu/dlcrest/api/core/bitstreams/120981f9-4a44-48e2-b930-1c7e726b2eb8/content)
- A 2019 critique, "Property rights regimes and natural resources: A conceptual analysis revisited" (UEA), revisits the schema's assumption of nested/cumulative rights. Relevance: allow non-nested bundles (e.g. lord holds alienation while tenant holds use + inherit). — [UEA eprints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/61963/)
- **Webster, C.J. & Lai, L.W.-C. (2003). *Property Rights, Planning and Markets: Managing Spontaneous Cities*. Edward Elgar.** [verified] Explains urban order as evolving from cooperation to cut transaction costs, distinguishing organisational, institutional, proprietary (fragmentation of property rights), spatial and public-domain order. Relevance: theoretical basis for making plot pattern an emergent outcome of rights allocation. — [Elgar](https://e-elgar.com/shop/gbp/property-rights-planning-and-markets-9781840649048.html); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/2334/)

### Inferences
- Encode rights as a matrix parcel × right → holder entity, with regime-change events rewriting columns; this supports both nested (Ostrom) and fragmented (Webster & Lai "proprietary order") cases.

### Gaps
- Alterman's comparative work on property rights/planning not verified in this session.

## Q4. US: PLSS, railroad/town-company platting and speculation

### Takeaway
Reps and Hudson document that US town form was largely produced by speculative, standardised platting along rail lines atop the PLSS grid — useful as a "plat-first, settle-later" growth mode.

### Cited Findings
- **Reps, J.W. (1965). *The Making of Urban America: A History of City Planning in the United States*. Princeton University Press.** [verified] Includes chapters "Gridiron cities and checkerboard plans" and "Cities for sale: land speculation in American planning"; 300+ reproduced plans. — [Princeton UP](https://press.princeton.edu/node/58550); [De Gruyter DOI 10.1515/9780691238241](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9780691238241/html)
- **Hudson, J.C. (1985). *Plains Country Towns*. University of Minnesota Press.** [verified] Studies ~20,000 sq mi of north-central North Dakota where, 1880–1920, railroad colonisation agents and speculators platted 500+ town sites, with the Soo Line, Northern Pacific and Great Northern placing towns at roughly ten-mile intervals. Relevance: rules for town-site spacing and railroad-company as initial holder of all lots. — [Great Plains Quarterly review](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/greatplainsquarterly/952)
- **Linklater, A. (2002). *Measuring America*. Walker & Co.** [verified] Popular history of how the land west of the Ohio was surveyed (Gunter's chain, PLSS) for sale to fund the young republic. Relevance: background for PLSS geometry (townships/sections) as a pre-existing cadastral lattice. — [Library record](https://library.usi.edu/record/261400)
- Speculative holding mechanics: see CHALMS (Q1). — [CoMSES](https://catalog.comses.net/publications/2419)

### Gaps
- No ABM of railroad town platting or PLSS-constrained growth was found.

## Q5. Medieval English burgage tenure, commons and enclosure

### Takeaway
Conzen's burgage cycle and Slater's plot metrology give concrete, modelable rules for long narrow burgage plots that fill, subdivide and redevelop; Beresford supplies the planted-town context.

### Cited Findings
- **Beresford, M.W. (1967). *New Towns of the Middle Ages: Town Plantation in England, Wales and Gascony*. London: Lutterworth (reissued 1988).** [verified] Seminal study of towns founded by kings/lords; regular burgage plots (burgesses held plots for an annual rent) and laid-out market places as hallmarks. — [Wikipedia on Beresford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Beresford); [Gatehouse text](https://gatehouse-gazetteer.info/Books/booktext/ntmamb.html)
- **Conzen, M.R.G. (1960). *Alnwick, Northumberland: A Study in Town-Plan Analysis*.** [concepts verified via secondary sources] Introduced the burgage cycle — progressive backland infill until saturation, clearance, "urban fallow", then redevelopment — plus fringe belt and morphological region; made the plot a fundamental morphological unit. Relevance: implement as a per-plot state machine. — [Journal of Urban Morphology](https://journal.urbanform.org/index.php/jum/article/download/3896/3218/13149); [Northumberland Archives: What is a burgage?](https://northumberlandarchives.com/?p=21640)
- **Slater, T.R.** (various, metrological analysis of plot boundaries). Measured plot widths to infer original surveyed widths and later subdivisions. Relevance: generate initial burgages from a unit width (e.g. perch multiples) and subdivide by halves/thirds. — [Journal of Urban Morphology source above](https://journal.urbanform.org/index.php/jum/article/download/3896/3218/13149)

### Gaps
- Hoskins (*The Making of the English Landscape*, 1955) and enclosure/encroachment literature not verified in this session; specific Slater citations (e.g. 1981 "The analysis of burgage patterns in medieval towns", *Area*) not confirmed.
